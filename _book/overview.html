Fantastic Genes and Where to Find Them

Overview

Throughout the semester, you will use a web-based visualization tool called a Genome Browser to explore gene structure and function in humans. We will focus on the human gene, BBS1. Mutations in BBS1 have been implicated in Bardet Biedl Syndrome, a pleiotropic1 disease with a variety of symptoms including blindness, obesity, polydactyly, cognitive impairment, renal abnormalities, infertility and anosmia. In addition to learning how to navigate through the genome browser, you will also learn how to

  1. Evaluate gene structure within a genome (Chapter 2)
  2. Use RNA evidence to pinpoint the position of genes (Chapters 3 and 4).
  3. Identify DNA and RNA sequence motifs critical for gene expression (Chapters 5 and 6).
  4. Identify and assess pathogenic and benign mutations (Chapter 7).
  5. Analyze gene sequence conservation between humans simpler model organisms (Chapter 8).

  1. pleiotropy is a condition in which a single gene influences more than one trait (Snustad).↩︎
